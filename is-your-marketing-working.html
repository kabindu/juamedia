How to tell if your marketing is actually working

Four numbers, one dashboard, and a monthly report your directors will actually read.

Dennis OtienoGoogle Ads & Analytics Sep 19, 2026 3 min read Share

On this page

  1. The four numbers
  2. Tracking them is the hard part, and it is mostly setup
  3. Build one screen, not twelve tabs
  4. The monthly report, in one page

Most marketing reports are long because length feels like value. They open with reach, impressions and follower growth, and somewhere on page six, if at all, they mention how many people got in touch. That order is backwards and it hides whether anything is working.

You need four numbers. Everything else is context for those four.

The four numbers

  1. Enquiries. Calls, forms and messages from people who want to buy. Not traffic, not reach. Enquiries.
  2. Cost per enquiry. Everything you spent that month, divided by the number above.
  3. Source. Which channel produced each enquiry, so you can spend more on what works.
  4. Closed business. How many became customers, and what they were worth.

If you can produce those four every month, you can run a marketing budget sensibly. If you cannot, you are guessing confidently.

Tracking them is the hard part, and it is mostly setup

The numbers are only as good as the plumbing. Before you can report anything honestly, these need to exist:

  • A tracked phone number, or call tracking on the website, so calls are attributed rather than assumed.
  • Form submissions firing a conversion event, confirmed by a thank-you page you can test.
  • WhatsApp links tagged by source, so a message from a TikTok advert is distinguishable from one from Google.
  • A simple record of what closed, even if it lives in a spreadsheet. Especially if it lives in a spreadsheet.
61%

of the small business sites we audit have no working enquiry tracking at all. The form emails an inbox and nothing is recorded.

Build one screen, not twelve tabs

A dashboard that takes effort to read does not get read. Ours fit on one screen: the four numbers, this month against last month, and a short list of which pages and campaigns produced the enquiries.

Looker Studio does this free and connects to Google Ads, Analytics and a Google Sheet, which is enough for most businesses. The point is not the tool. The point is that anyone in the business can glance at it and know whether the month is going well.

If the report needs explaining, it is not a report. It is homework.

The monthly report, in one page

We send clients a single page every month with four sections, in this order:

  1. What happened: the four numbers, with last month beside them.
  2. What we did: the work that was carried out, briefly.
  3. What we learned: one or two things the data changed our mind about.
  4. What is next: what we will do in the coming month, and why.

That is enough for a director to read in three minutes and ask a good question about. Anything longer gets filed unread, and an unread report is the same as no report at all.

What to take away

  • Four numbers: enquiries, cost per enquiry, source, closed business.
  • Fix the plumbing first. Untracked calls and messages make every report fiction.
  • One screen, not twelve tabs. A dashboard nobody opens is worthless.
  • One page a month: what happened, what we did, what we learned, what is next.

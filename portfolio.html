Google Ads · SEO

From wasted clicks to a full enquiry book

Same budget, rebuilt around the forty keywords that actually sell.

+312%more enquiries
9Months of work
2026Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientSokoni Hardware
  • SectorHardware & building supplies
  • WhereNairobi CBD
  • ServicesGoogle Ads, SEO, conversion tracking
  • Timeframe9 months

The situation

Sokoni had been advertising on Google for two years and could not say whether it worked. The account spent a steady amount every month, the clicks came in, and the counter staff had no idea which customers had arrived that way.

When we read the search terms report for the first time, roughly a third of the spend was going to searches that could never buy: job seekers, students researching coursework, and shoppers in other countries comparing prices.

What we did

  1. Added conversion tracking for forms, calls and WhatsApp before changing anything, so there was a baseline to judge against.
  2. Built a negative keyword list from twelve months of search terms, which removed the job and coursework traffic in week one.
  3. Collapsed six thin campaigns into two with enough budget to learn from.
  4. Wrote separate pages for the four product lines that actually sell, and pointed each ad group at its own page.
  5. Set location targeting to presence only, so the budget stayed in the counties they deliver to.

What happened

Enquiries more than quadrupled on a budget that did not change. Cost per enquiry fell by just over two thirds, because the same money was finally reaching people who wanted to buy building supplies.

The more useful outcome was less dramatic: the counter team can now tell the owner which products the adverts are producing demand for, so stock decisions and advertising decisions are made in the same conversation.

Three months in we were paying less and getting three times the enquiries.
WK Wanjiru KamauOwner, Sokoni Hardware

What we would do next

The next step is their own product pages for the slower-moving lines, so the SEO work can carry traffic the advertising currently pays for.

Website · Instagram

Direct bookings instead of agent commission

A booking site plus an Instagram programme built on guest photography.

+68%direct bookings
7Months of work
2025Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientMara Breeze Camp
  • SectorHospitality & tourism
  • WhereNarok
  • ServicesWebsite build, Instagram, direct booking
  • Timeframe7 months

The situation

Almost every booking arrived through an agent, with the commission that implies. The camp had a website, but it was slow, built for desktop, and had no way to take a booking without an email exchange.

Guests were already photographing the place constantly. None of it was being used.

What we did

  1. Rebuilt the site as a fast, mobile-first booking site, tested on mobile data rather than office wifi.
  2. Put rates, availability and what is included on the page, instead of behind an enquiry form.
  3. Set up a guest photography programme: permission, a simple brief, and a monthly posting rhythm.
  4. Ran Instagram as a destination account rather than a brochure, posting four times a week.
  5. Tagged every booking by source so agent and direct bookings could finally be compared.

What happened

Direct bookings rose by 68 per cent over three quarters. Because a direct booking carries no agent commission, the margin improvement was larger than the booking numbers alone suggest.

The camp still works with agents, and deliberately so. The difference is that it is now a choice rather than the only option.

Bookings started coming through the website in the first month.
PO Peter OtienoGeneral Manager, Mara Breeze Camp

What we would do next

A returning-guest programme, because the cheapest booking a camp can get is the one from somebody who has already been.

Local SEO · Google

Four branches, four full appointment books

Found on Maps in every neighbourhood they serve.

1,400appointments booked
12Months of work
2026Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientAfya Bora Clinics
  • SectorHealth & clinics
  • WhereNairobi, four branches
  • ServicesLocal SEO, Google Ads, Google Business Profiles
  • Timeframe12 months

The situation

Four branches shared a single Google Business Profile with the head office address on it. Three of the four were effectively invisible to anyone searching nearby.

The website listed all four under a Locations tab, which no search engine treated as four separate places of business.

What we did

  1. Created and verified a separate Google Business Profile for each branch, with its own address, phone number and hours.
  2. Built a real page per branch, with its own photographs, its own services and its own directions.
  3. Set the primary category to the most specific option available for each branch rather than a generic one.
  4. Introduced a review routine at discharge, asked by the person who had just treated the patient.
  5. Ran small, tightly targeted ad campaigns for each branch's immediate neighbourhood.

What happened

Around 1,400 appointments were booked through search across the four branches over the year, where previously only the head office branch appeared at all.

The branches that gained most were the two furthest from the centre, which had been invisible for years despite serving dense residential areas.

The leads from Google Maps alone paid for the whole retainer.
GM Grace MutuaOperations Director, Afya Bora Clinics

What we would do next

Appointment booking directly from the profile, so the patient never has to reach the website at all.

Branding · Social

Loan products explained in plain Kiswahili

Dense product sheets turned into short explainer videos.

2,100new members
10Months of work
2024Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientTumaini Sacco
  • SectorSaccos & financial services
  • WhereNakuru
  • ServicesBranding, social media, plain-language content
  • Timeframe10 months

The situation

Tumaini's loan products were explained in the language of a regulatory filing. Members asked the same questions at the counter every week because nothing written down answered them.

The social accounts posted motivational quotes and anniversary graphics, and almost nothing about what the sacco actually offered.

What we did

  1. Rewrote every product in plain Kiswahili and plain English, with the cost stated in shillings rather than percentages alone.
  2. Built a one-page brand guide the branches could actually follow, including minimum logo sizes for signage.
  3. Replaced the quote graphics with short videos answering the questions the counter staff were asked most.
  4. Set up a WhatsApp enquiry line with saved replies for the eight most common questions.
  5. Trained two branch staff to film and post, so the programme did not depend on us.

What happened

Around 2,100 new members joined across the period, roughly double the preceding year. The clearest signal was in the enquiry mix: questions moved from 'what is this product' to 'how do I apply'.

Counter staff reported noticeably shorter conversations, because the explaining had already happened before the member arrived.

Members stopped asking us what the products were and started asking how to join.
JC Josephine ChepkoechMarketing Officer, Tumaini Sacco

What we would do next

Member stories on video, which the sacco can now film without us.

TikTok · Launch

A snack launch that reached two retail chains

Short video, activations and sampling across thirty outlets.

1.9Mvideo views
5Months of work
2025Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientPwani Foods
  • SectorFood manufacturing
  • WhereMombasa
  • ServicesTikTok, product launch, influencer matching
  • Timeframe5 months

The situation

A new snack line with no distribution and no budget for traditional advertising. The product was good and nobody had heard of it.

The brief was uncomfortable but clear: create enough visible demand that retail chains would take the call.

What we did

  1. Built a TikTok account around the factory rather than the product, because how food is made is more interesting than the packet.
  2. Filmed in short bursts on site, twice a month, producing four weeks of content each time.
  3. Matched eight small Kenyan creators to the product by audience rather than follower count.
  4. Answered every comment asking where to buy, which was the only call to action used.
  5. Kept a public count of stockists, which gave viewers a reason to keep checking back.

What happened

The account reached 1.9 million views in five months, almost entirely organic. More importantly, two retail chains made contact, and both cited the visible demand as the reason.

The comments doubled as market research. The most requested flavour had not been in the original range and is now in production.

We walked into those meetings with evidence instead of a pitch deck.
HM Hassan MwinyiFounder, Pwani Foods

What we would do next

Supporting the retail listings with in-store sampling, filmed and posted the same day.

Website · SEO

An admissions pipeline that runs itself

Parents find the school, check fees and enquire online.

310admission leads
8Months of work
2024Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientBaraka Academy
  • SectorSchools & education
  • WhereKiambu
  • ServicesWebsite rebuild, SEO, admissions funnel
  • Timeframe8 months

The situation

Admissions ran on word of mouth and a banner at the gate. The website had not been updated in three years and did not say the fees.

Parents researching schools were finding competitors and not finding Baraka, despite better results.

What we did

  1. Rebuilt the site around the questions parents actually ask, with fees, term dates and transport routes stated plainly.
  2. Wrote a page for each of the questions that came up repeatedly at open days.
  3. Set up a simple enquiry form that created a tracked record rather than an email nobody owned.
  4. Published exam results and staff qualifications, which competitors in the area were not doing.
  5. Timed content to the admissions calendar rather than posting evenly through the year.

What happened

310 admission enquiries were tracked over the period, from a baseline of effectively zero measurable online enquiries.

Publishing the fees was the change the school resisted most and the one that moved the needle furthest. Enquiries fell in volume briefly and rose sharply in quality.

We were afraid to publish fees. It turned out to be the thing parents wanted most.
DM Daniel MwangiHead Teacher, Baraka Academy

What we would do next

A parent referral programme, since word of mouth was already the strongest channel before any of this.

Landing pages · Ads

Site visits booked while the ad is still running

A page per development feeding WhatsApp follow-up.

−37%cost per site visit
6Months of work
2025Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientJengo Properties
  • SectorProperty & land
  • WhereNairobi & Kiambu
  • ServicesLanding pages, Google & Meta ads
  • Timeframe6 months

The situation

Every advert pointed at the homepage, which listed eleven developments. Prospective buyers had to find the one they had clicked for, and most did not.

Cost per site visit had been climbing for a year and nobody could explain why.

What we did

  1. Built a dedicated landing page per development, each with its own photographs, price range, payment plan and map.
  2. Matched the advert headline to the page headline word for word.
  3. Added a site visit booking form with three fields instead of nine.
  4. Tracked bookings as the conversion rather than form views.
  5. Paused the two developments that were absorbing budget and producing nothing.

What happened

Cost per booked site visit fell 37 per cent in six months with no increase in budget. The three-field form accounted for more of that than the pages did.

The decision to pause two developments was the least popular and the most profitable. The budget moved to the three that were already converting.

Shorter form, matching page. We should have done it years ago.
AN Alice NjokiSales Director, Jengo Properties

What we would do next

Video walkthroughs on each landing page, which buyers keep asking for on the phone.

Home›Client results›Rafiki Motors

Shopping · Social

Every vehicle in stock, advertised automatically

Stock feeds into Shopping and catalogue ads, with retargeting.

4.1xreturn on ad spend
11Months of work
2026Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientRafiki Motors
  • SectorAutomotive
  • WhereNairobi
  • ServicesShopping campaigns, paid social, feed automation
  • Timeframe11 months

The situation

Stock changed weekly and the website never kept up. Adverts regularly sent buyers to vehicles that had already sold, which wasted the click and annoyed the buyer.

Listing each vehicle manually was a job nobody had time for, so most of the forecourt was never advertised at all.

What we did

  1. Connected the stock list to an automated product feed, so every vehicle advertises itself the day it arrives.
  2. Set sold vehicles to drop out of the adverts automatically rather than waiting for someone to notice.
  3. Ran Shopping campaigns on the feed instead of writing individual adverts.
  4. Added short video walkarounds for the higher-value stock, filmed on a phone on the forecourt.
  5. Tracked enquiries per vehicle, so pricing decisions had evidence behind them.

What happened

Return on ad spend reached 4.1 times by the fourth quarter, from 1.6 at the start. Most of the gain came from advertising the whole forecourt rather than the handful of vehicles someone had found time to list.

Wasted clicks on sold stock fell to almost nothing, which improved both the numbers and the showroom's patience with advertising.

Every car on the yard is now advertising itself the day it lands.
SK Samuel KariukiDealer Principal, Rafiki Motors

What we would do next

Finance pre-qualification on the vehicle pages, so enquiries arrive further along.

E-commerce · Brand

Farm produce delivered across Nairobi estates

An online shop with M-Pesa checkout and delivery zones.

640orders a month
9Months of work
2025Completed
0Accounts we kept

At a glance

  • ClientKilimo Fresh
  • SectorAgribusiness & delivery
  • WhereNairobi estates
  • ServicesEcommerce build, brand, M-Pesa checkout
  • Timeframe9 months

The situation

Orders arrived through WhatsApp messages and were written in a notebook. It worked until it did not, and it could not grow past the number of messages one person could answer.

There was no website, no price list and no way to order outside working hours.

What we did

  1. Built a simple online shop with M-Pesa as the first and largest payment option.
  2. Published delivery costs by estate on the product page, not at checkout.
  3. Photographed the actual produce weekly rather than using stock images.
  4. Kept WhatsApp as a channel but connected it to the same order record.
  5. Added one abandoned cart reminder, sent within the hour on WhatsApp.

What happened

Orders reached 640 a month, and roughly a fifth of them are placed outside working hours, which was impossible before.

Publishing delivery costs per estate removed the single most common reason people had been abandoning the WhatsApp conversation.

People order at ten at night now. We just see it in the morning.
MW Mercy WairimuFounder, Kilimo Fresh

What we would do next

A weekly subscription box, which existing customers have already asked for twice.
